## Five closest design constraints

1. **FinBalance and FinED-Bench are 2026 direct competitors.** Any novelty claim must be narrower than multi-document accounting reconciliation or financial document error detection.
2. **FinDVer already studies explainable financial claim verification.** Citation/evidence trace alone is not a defensible new contribution. ACL abstract (4,000/four), arXiv abstract (2,400/three), and ACL PDF body (2,100 initial/three) disagree; the author README confirms three subsets and a later test-label release but gives no resolving total. Exact count remains unresolved.
3. **Selective classification and conformal risk control are established methods.** A financial application is not novel merely because it adds a reject/review action.
4. **Temporal and database provenance ideas are established.** A graph of source, valid time, recorded time and supersession is a design requirement, not itself a novelty claim.
5. **Generator independence and source-/time-shift evaluation are requirements for evidence, not evidence of novelty.** Synthetic scores cannot validate a bank workflow.

## Coverage interpretation

The closest current work is not a single paper. FinBalance and FinAuditing cover multi-document financial audit/reconciliation; FinED-Bench and FIND cover document error detection; FinDVer, RFC-Bench and When FEVER Meets Finance cover financial claim/paragraph verification; FinDoc-RAG, HC-RAG/Multi-Doc-2025, FinFIRST and TempFinRAG cover evidence-grounded financial QA and source provenance; EvidenceLens covers claim-evidence matrix visualization and review prioritization; FinAgent demonstrates the need for auditable gold/version correction. The proposed borrower/case-level separation of comparability, relation, attribution uncertainty and review action may remain a benchmark-design distinction, but it has not been established as novel.
