# Literature and COBI application

The papers below motivate standard methods and limits. COBI applies them in a prototype and a single public benchmark.

| Reference and problem | Mathematical method | Scientific contribution | COBI application | Remaining limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lessmann, Baesens, Seow & Thomas (2015). *Benchmarking state-of-the-art classification algorithms for credit scoring: An update of research*. EJOR 247(1), 124–136. [Accepted manuscript](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/377196/1/Lessmann_Benchmarking.pdf) | Compare 41 classifiers on eight credit datasets and multiple performance criteria, with statistical comparisons. | Model comparisons depend on dataset and metric. | Keep dummy and logistic baselines alongside trees and MLP. | One UCI cohort cannot generalize the ranking to other portfolios. |
| Niculescu-Mizil & Caruana (2005). *Predicting Good Probabilities with Supervised Learning*. ICML, 625–632. [ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/1102351.1102430) | Compare predicted scores with empirical frequencies, fit sigmoid or isotonic maps on calibration observations. | Documents algorithm-specific probability distortion and the role of calibration data size and method. | Use a separate sigmoid cohort and retain raw/calibrated test diagnostics. | A fitted map can worsen probability quality on another sample. |
| Wang & Strong (1996). *Beyond Accuracy: What Data Quality Means to Data Consumers*. JMIS 12(4), 5–33. [Journal](https://www.jmis-web.org/articles/1002) | Surveys, sorting and hierarchical categorization of consumer-reported data quality attributes. | Distinguishes intrinsic, contextual, representational and accessibility dimensions. | Keep evidence coverage, consistency, recency and verification visible. | COBI’s weights are policy assumptions. The paper does not validate them. |
| Verbraken, Bravo, Weber & Baesens (2014). *Development and application of consumer credit scoring models using profit-based classification measures*. EJOR 238(2), 505–513. [Journal article](https://repositorio.uchile.cl/bitstream/handle/2250/126710/Development-and-application-of-consumer-credit-scoring-models-using-profit-based-classification-measures.pdf) | Expected Maximum Profit uses costs and benefits to evaluate score thresholds. | Accuracy and expected economic value can lead to different cutoff choices. | Expose exposure, loss and review-cost assumptions and sensitivity. | COBI lacks measured bank economics and an approved loss model. |
| Mozannar & Sontag (2020). *Consistent Estimators for Learning to Defer to an Expert*. ICML, PMLR 119, 7076–7087. [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/mozannar20b.html) | Joint classifier and deferral policy with a consistent surrogate for system error under expert decisions. | Deferral is part of a human-plus-model system and depends on expert performance. | Make review and escalation explicit. | COBI uses fixed gates and has no learned deferral model or expert outcome labels. |
| Lakkaraju, Kleinberg, Leskovec, Ludwig & Mullainathan (2017). *The Selective Labels Problem: Evaluating Algorithmic Predictions in the Presence of Unobservables*. KDD, 275–284. [Author copy](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/home/kleinber/kdd17-selective.pdf) | Contraction exploits variation across decision-makers when outcomes are only observed after selected actions. | Conventional holdout accuracy can mislead when decisions determine which labels become visible. | Record eligibility, decision and outcome availability in the pilot. | A benchmark on UCI does not address selective labels in HLBVN. |
| Kozodoi, Lessmann, Alamgir, Moreira-Matias & Papakonstantinou (2025). *Fighting sampling bias: A framework for training and evaluating credit scoring models*. EJOR 324(2), 616–628. [Journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2025.01.040) · [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13009) | Bias-aware self-learning for reject inference and Bayesian evaluation under label-selection bias, including trial-based holdout evidence. | Demonstrates why evaluating the broader applicant pool needs an explicit sampling design. | Specify observation and rejection handling before bank validation. | No reject inference, randomized label collection or profitability estimate in COBI. |
| Kozodoi, Jacob & Lessmann (2022). *Fairness in credit scoring: Assessment, implementation and profit implications*. EJOR 297(3), 1083–1094. [Publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377221721005385) | Empirical comparison of fairness criteria and mitigation under a profit objective. | Fairness metrics and profitability can move differently. | Demographic exclusion is one input design choice. Independently measure subgroup error, calibration and operational outcomes before a bank pilot. | No fairness or profit trade-off was estimated in this release. |
| Lundberg et al. (2020). *From local explanations to global understanding with explainable AI for trees*. Nature Machine Intelligence 2, 56–67. [Journal](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-019-0138-9) | TreeSHAP computes additive tree-model attributions and aggregates local explanations. | Local explanations can describe what a fitted tree model relies on. | Explain the persisted XGBoost on validation with additivity checks. | Attribution is observational and sensitive to correlated and grouped predictors. |
| Van Calster et al. (2019). *Calibration: the Achilles heel of predictive analytics*. BMC Medicine 17, 230. [Open article](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-019-1466-7) | Calibration intercept, slope and curves across levels of agreement. | Useful discrimination does not ensure probabilities match observed outcomes. | Report slope, intercept, fixed-bin counts, observed-to-expected and proper scores. | Calibration checks are conditional on this cohort and measurement design. |

For training mechanics, cite Chen & Guestrin (2016), *XGBoost: A Scalable Tree Boosting System*, KDD, 785–794. [Author paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.02754).
